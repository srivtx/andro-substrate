# How many framework methods a real app calls, and what that number is not

**Status:** measured. Six pure-DEX F-Droid APKs on an Android 13 (`android-33`,
`arm64-v8a`, `default` image) x86-free arm64 emulator, macOS host, ART method
tracing. 76 capture slots, **64 usable**, 12 recorded as failures. Every number
below is regenerated by `trace/measure.mjs` into `trace/REPORT.md`, which is the
artefact of the run this document describes.

Every figure is labelled **exhaustive** (every method entry recorded) or
**sampled@N µs (lower bound)** (a subset; ART named only the methods a sampling
tick happened to land inside). Nothing here is a lower bound presented as a
measurement.

---

The shim in ADR 0005 declares 144 classes / 334 methods against a *declared*
platform of 6 757 methods (`analysis/candidates.md`). These numbers are a
different quantity and must not be divided into it: this is what **these six
apps** touched, measured on a **re-creation** of an already-warm activity, not
what any app in general needs, and not what the platform declares.

## 2. Per app — `recreate` mode, 3 runs each

`recreate` is the method the earlier numbers were taken with: profile, then
`am start --activity-clear-task`. It counts creation **and** teardown; §3
removes the teardown part by measurement rather than assertion.

| package | coverage | per-run framework methods | union | \|union of k runs\|, mean over all 6 orderings | pairwise Jaccard (min) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tk.al54.dev.badpixels` 4 | exhaustive | 3 866, 3 871, 3 862 | **3 875** | 3 866.3 → 3 870.7 → 3 875.0 | 0.9966 |
| `de.cweiske.headphoneindicator` 3 | exhaustive | 4 430, 4 412, 4 405 | **4 432** | 4 415.7 → 4 425.0 → 4 432.0 | 0.9944 |
| `eu.quelltext.gita` 6 | exhaustive | 4 638, 4 638, 4 637 | **4 638** | 4 637.7 → 4 638.0 → 4 638.0 | 0.9998 |
| `com.jeffliu.balancetheball` 4 | exhaustive | 4 605, 4 619, 4 631 | **4 738** | 4 618.3 → 4 698.0 → 4 738.0 | 0.9498 |
| `org.billthefarmer.shorty` 116 | exhaustive | 6 255, 6 100, 6 100 | **6 263** | 6 151.7 → 6 208.7 → 6 263.0 | 0.9727 |
| `com.oF2pks.applicationsinfo` 19 | **TRUNCATED** (overflow 3/3) | 6 523, 6 527, 6 202 | *6 569 — lower bound* | *6 417.3 → 6 553.7 → 6 569.0* | *0.9386* |

The italicised row is **not** a measurement of `applicationsinfo`'s surface. ART's
trace ring buffer filled during the clear-task burst and dropped entries
(`data-file-overflow=true` in all three runs), so the method table is a subset of
what ran. Re-capturing with a shorter window does not fix it — at
`--window 2` it still overflows, and at `--window 3` it overflows *and* reports
3 473 distinct methods, **fewer** than the 6 597 the longer window reported. A
truncated count is therefore not even a stable lower bound; it depends on where
the buffer happened to fill. See §6.8. The 5 non-overflowing apps are the
defensible ones: **3 875 – 6 263, median 4 638**.

**The convergence curve is flat after the first run.** Across the 5 apps whose
traces did not overflow, the mean third run adds between 0.0 % (`gita`:
4 637.7 → 4 638.0) and 2.6 % (`balancetheball`) to the union. Three exhaustive
runs is enough for these apps; it would not follow for an app whose surface
depends on data or network state, and the harness does not assume it —
`measure.mjs` reports the curve for whatever runs exist and enumerates every
ordering, so the figure cannot be an artefact of which run was captured first.

## 3. The teardown confound, quantified

**The claim being tested.** `am start --activity-clear-task` re-creates the
activity and, in the same profiling window, destroys the previous one. Both
halves are counted, so the `recreate` total includes teardown methods a first
launch would never call.

**How it is measured, not guessed.** `trace/capture.sh --mode create` finishes
the activity with BACK *before* profiling starts, verifies via
`dumpsys activity activities` that the app is no longer the resumed activity, and
only then records a creation — so that window has a creation and no teardown.
`--mode teardown` records BACK alone. `--mode idle` records a window in which the
app is already resumed and nothing is re-created: the noise floor.

`R` = `recreate` union, `C` = `create` union, both exhaustive, both unions over
their own runs.

| package | R (`recreate`) | C (`create`) | idle | teardown (BACK) | **R minus C** | share of R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tk.al54.dev.badpixels` 4 | 3 875 | 2 900 | 91 | 1 898 | **1 645** | **42.5 %** |
| `eu.quelltext.gita` 6 | 4 638 | 2 437 | 91 | 1 883 | **2 531** | **54.6 %** |
| `de.cweiske.headphoneindicator` 3 | 4 432 | 1 770 | 91 | 1 882 | **2 692** | **60.7 %** |
| `org.billthefarmer.shorty` 116 | 6 263 | 2 327 | 138 | 2 492 | **3 964** | **63.3 %** |
| `com.oF2pks.applicationsinfo` 19 | *6 569 (R truncated)* | 2 269 | 151 | 3 496 | *4 574 — **understated**, see below* | *69.6 %* |
| `com.jeffliu.balancetheball` 4 | 4 738 | — | 379 | — | **not measurable** | — |

**Answer to "what is the teardown confound worth, in methods, per app?":**
**1 645 to 4 574 methods, 42.5 % to 69.6 % of what the `--activity-clear-task`
method counts**, over the 5 apps that admit the decomposition. Median of those 5
is **2 692** (`headphoneindicator`). Restricting to the 4 apps whose `R` did not
overflow — the ones the number is actually sound for — the range is
**1 645 – 3 964** (42.5 % – 63.3 %), median **2 611.5**.

**The two bias directions are opposite, and both are stated.**

*For the 5 non-overflowing apps, `R minus C` is an upper bound.* `C` is a *warm*
re-creation — the second activity this process has built, with its classes
already loaded — whereas `R`'s creation is the first, so `R` legitimately
contains cold-start paths (class loading, first-time initialisation) that `C`
never calls. Those land in `R minus C` and are counted here as if they were
teardown. Tightening the bound needs a *cold* creation-only trace, which §6
records as not obtainable in the batched layout on this image.

*For `applicationsinfo` the same figure is an under-estimate, for a different
reason.* Its `R` is truncated by buffer overflow, so `R` is missing methods that
really ran. Its `C` is **not** truncated (all 3 `create` runs report
`data-file-overflow=false`), so the difference is missing `R` entries. The
4 574 is a floor, not a ceiling, and the 69.6 % share is not a measurement of
anything. Its `teardown` union of 3 496 is likewise truncated (overflow in 1 of
3 runs), and the two lowest `teardown` runs of that app (2 359, 2 364) are the
ones consistent with the other apps, which is what a spill looks like.

**The independent estimate does not agree, and that is reported rather than
resolved.** The `teardown` column comes from a *different* trigger (BACK, which
runs `onStop`/`onDestroy` and window teardown) and lands at 1 883–3 496. Its
agreement with `R minus C` is good for three apps and poor for two
(`gita`: 2 531 vs 1 883; `applicationsinfo`: 4 574 vs 3 496). BACK-teardown and
clear-task-teardown are not the same code path, so the smaller of the two is not
a lower bound on the larger. `measure.mjs` records
`teardownVsAttributableJaccard` for exactly this reason.

`com.jeffliu.balancetheball` is **not comparable** and is not given a number.
It consumes the BACK key — `dumpsys` shows its activity still resumed after four
attempts — so no creation-only window can be established for it. Its `create`
runs are recorded as `failed-precondition-not-destroyed`, not as data. An earlier
version of the harness produced 379-method `create`/`teardown`/`idle` traces for
it that were in fact idle traces wearing the wrong label; the precondition check
exists because of that.

## 4. Is an unsampled trace obtainable on this system image?

**Yes. The earlier conclusion — that `am profile start` without `--sampling`
produces 0 bytes and full traces are therefore impossible — is wrong, and the
cause is a flush-timing artefact, not an inability.**

`am profile start` returns immediately and the target file is **0 bytes for the
whole profiling window**. ART buffers the trace in the target process and writes
it when profiling stops. Measured on `eu.quelltext.gita`:

```
size right after start:  0
size before stop:        0
size after stop:         7708140
```

A reader that pulls before `am profile stop` sees an empty file. **You must call
`am profile stop <pkg>` before you look at the file.** The `default` system image
is not Play-protected, `adb root` works, and the device is uid 0, so
`/data/local/tmp` is writable by the traced process. No device modification was
needed.

### `--start-profiler` does work, but only with `--streaming`, and it is unreliable

| invocation | bytes | why |
|---|---|---|
| `am start --start-profiler F -n C` | **0** | ART buffers and flushes on a clean process exit, which an Android app never performs. `am force-stop` is SIGKILL; so is `SIGTERM` (both measured). |
| `am start --start-profiler F --streaming -n C` | **8 388 599** | streaming flushes incrementally, so the file exists while the app runs |
| `am start --start-profiler F --streaming --sampling 100 -n C` | **0** | sampled + streaming is broken on this image |
| `am profile start --sampling 100 P F` … `am profile stop P` | works | buffered, flushed on stop |
| `am profile start P F` … `am profile stop P` | works | same, **exhaustive** |

`--start-profiler` is a valid `am start` option and is documented in `am`'s own
usage. The 0-byte cases are silent: `am` reports `Starting: Intent {…}` and exits
0, `pm path` still succeeds, and the file exists with the right name.

**The streaming layout is a different format and needs a different parser.** It
has no `*version`/`*methods` preamble at all; method names are inline in the
record stream. `trace/parse.py` supports it (`layout: continuous`, discriminated
by the file starting `SLOW` rather than `*version`).

**`--start-profiler --streaming` succeeded for only 2 of 6 apps** (4 of 12 run
slots; 8 recorded `failed-zero-bytes`). It is the only way found to observe a
**cold, from-process-start, teardown-free** launch, and on this image it cannot
be relied on:

| package | `startprof` union (cold, from process start) | coverage |
|---|---|---|
| `eu.quelltext.gita` 6 | **6 025** (2 runs, Jaccard 0.9973) | **overflow NOT verifiable** — continuous layout carries no flag |
| `com.oF2pks.applicationsinfo` 19 | **8 904** (2 runs, Jaccard 0.9933) | **overflow NOT verifiable**, and the two files are 32.0 MiB and 40.0 MiB, far past the 8.4–9.0 MiB at which this app's batched captures overflow (§6.8) — treat as a lower bound, and note that a 40.0 MiB file is exactly 40 × 2²⁰, i.e. a flush boundary, so run 2 is cut at a buffer edge |
| other 4 apps | **failed-zero-bytes**, excluded from every count | — |

These two figures are higher than any `recreate` figure in §2, which is the
expected direction: they start at process creation and therefore include
`Application.onCreate`, class loading and the first, cold activity creation — none
of which `recreate` sees. `applicationsinfo`'s `startprof` is 2 335 methods above
its `recreate` union (8 904 vs 6 569) — though that subtraction mixes a possibly
truncated right-hand side (§6.8) with a left-hand side whose truncation cannot be
checked at all, so it is an indication and not a measurement. Both figures are
**lower bounds on a cold launch**, because the streaming file is a prefix of a
live stream: it is cut where the capture window ended, and the last record may be
torn (dropped by the parser).

**Answer: an unsampled trace *is* obtainable, two ways.** `am profile start` with
no `--sampling`, plus `am profile stop` before reading the file, is reliable and
gives the batched layout. `am start --start-profiler … --streaming` gives a cold
from-process-start trace in the continuous layout, worked for 2 of 6 apps, and
**its failures are silent and must be checked for.**

## 5. Sampling: what interval buys what coverage

Same app, same capture mode (`recreate`), one sampled@100 µs run against the
exhaustive run. `applicationsinfo` is **excluded** — its exhaustive-side run is
truncated (§6.8), so the ratio would compare a good lower bound to a bad one:

| package | sampled@100 µs | exhaustive | sampled as % of exhaustive | exhaustive / sampled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `org.billthefarmer.shorty` 116 | 254 | 6 255 | **4.1 %** | 24.6× |
| `com.jeffliu.balancetheball` 4 | 229 | 4 605 | **5.0 %** | 20.1× |
| `eu.quelltext.gita` 6 | 230 | 4 638 | **5.0 %** | 20.2× |
| `tk.al54.dev.badpixels` 4 | 195 | 3 866 | **5.0 %** | 19.8× |
| `de.cweiske.headphoneindicator` 3 | 329 | 4 430 | **7.4 %** | 13.5× |
| *n = 5* | | | *median **5.0 %*** | *median **20.1×*** |

(`applicationsinfo`'s own 1 099 sampled against its truncated 6 202 reads as
16.8 %, which is why it is not in the table: a truncated denominator flatters the
ratio, and the one app where sampling looked least lossy is the one app whose
exhaustive number is least trustworthy.)

**Answer to "if not [unsampled], what sampling interval do we need to claim a
given coverage?": the question does not arise on this image, because exhaustive
traces are obtainable (§4).** That is the correct answer, and the brief's
premise was wrong. What the table above does establish is the size of the error
that the earlier sampled numbers carried:

> **A 100 µs sampled trace recovers a median of 5.0 % of the framework methods
> an exhaustive trace of the same window records — a median 20.1× undercount,
> range 13.5× to 24.6×, over 5 apps.**

Mechanism: ART's sampling thread records whichever method each thread is
*inside* at a tick. A method whose entire body is shorter than the interval, and
which no call to it overlaps a tick, is never named, so it never enters the
method table. The table of a sampled trace is a strict subset of the exhaustive
one.

If a sampled trace is the only option, the interval must be chosen against a
known per-method duration, and **no interval can be justified from this data**:
that would need the duration distribution, which the method *table* does not
contain. What §5 supports is only the negative claim — 100 µs is far too coarse
by more than an order of magnitude, and the undercount is not uniform across
apps (4.1 %–16.8 %, a 4× spread), so it cannot be corrected by a single constant.

Two consequences, both of which changed numbers already taken:

- The earlier "methods common to all apps" figure computed from 100 µs samples is
  **not** a coverage measurement. On these same apps, the sampled `recreate`
  intersection is **75** methods against **3 515** for the 5 non-truncated
  exhaustive captures — a factor of **46.9**. (Against the 6-app figure of 3 203,
  which includes one truncated app, the factor is 42.7.) The intersection is the
  quantity most damaged by sampling, because a method must be hit in *every* app
  to appear, so per-app coverage errors compound.
- Sampling biases toward **long** methods. An intersection of long methods is not
  a floor on the surface any shim needs: it under-represents exactly the short
  accessors (`getX`, `setX`, trivial getters) that dominate a shim's registry.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Six apps, all F-Droid pure-DEX single-`classes.dex`, chosen for having a
   launcher activity and a small APK.** Not a random sample, and not
   representative of the corpus. `pro.rudloff.search_to_browser` and
   `com.oF2pks.neolinker` have activities but no `LAUNCHER` intent filter, so
   `cmd package resolve-activity --brief` returns nothing and `capture.sh` exits
   with an error rather than guessing a component. Six of six are
   `targetSdk ≤ 28`; **no app here exercises Android 10–13 behaviour changes**,
   scoped storage, or the `ActivityThread` paths that differ on modern versions.
2. **`recreate` is not "before the first frame".** It is a *re-creation* in a warm
   process. It misses process start, `Application.onCreate`, class loading, and
   the first cold creation. §2's numbers are an upper bound on a first launch for
   the *cold* component and an over-count for the teardown component; §3 measures
   the over-count, and the net is not a first-frame number.
3. **`R minus C` over-attributes** cold-start-only methods to teardown for the 4
   apps whose `R` did not overflow, and **under-attributes** for the 1 whose `R`
   did, because a truncated `R` is missing methods that really ran (§3, §6.8).
   Both directions are known; neither magnitude is bounded by this data, so the
   5-app range 1 645 – 4 574 should be read as "somewhere in this band", not as a
   point estimate with a confidence interval.
4. **`create` and `teardown` both rely on BACK.** It is racy in an emulator and
   one app of six consumes it outright. Both modes assert the precondition and
   record a failure when it does not hold, so a bad run is a recorded failure
   rather than a mislabelled trace — but the honest count of usable `create` runs
   is 15 of 17, and 15 of 17 for `teardown`.
5. **`--start-profiler --streaming` failed for 4 of 6 apps** (§4), so the
   from-process-start measurement exists for 2 apps and is a prefix of a live
   stream. It is a lower bound on a cold launch.
6. **"Framework" is a namespace prefix list** in `parse.py`
   (`FRAMEWORK_PREFIXES`, minus `FRAMEWORK_PREFIX_EXCEPTIONS`). It is not derived
   from `/system/framework`. `measure.mjs` prints the observed namespaces so the
   choice is auditable: the traces contain `android`, `java`, `javax`, `jdk`,
   `sun`, `libcore`, `dalvik`, `com.android.{internal,icu,org}` — all kept — plus
   the app's own packages — all dropped. No third-party library namespace
   (Kotlin, OkHttp) was observed in these six; **an app bundling a library would
   have that library counted as *app* code**, which is the correct reading of
   "framework" but means the app/framework split is a convention.
7. **Emulator, not hardware.** `-gpu swiftshader_indirect`, headless, no vsync.
   The framework surface of a *first frame* is timing-dependent and no
   first-frame boundary is located by this harness at all — `windowGainedFocus`,
   `Choreographer` callbacks and `RenderThread` work are all inside the window.
8. **ART's trace ring buffer overflowed on 8 of the 64 usable captures**, and the
   harness now says so on the face of the report
   (`TRUNCATED (data-file-overflow in n/m run(s)) — lower bound`). All 3
   `applicationsinfo` `recreate` runs, 1 of 3 `applicationsinfo` `teardown` runs,
   and both `startprof` runs of `gita` and of `applicationsinfo` (the latter two
   are the continuous layout, which carries **no** overflow flag at all, so their
   truncation is not merely detected but *unverifiable* — `parse.py` reports
   `null` and `measure.mjs` refuses to call them exhaustive).
   Three consequences, all of which change numbers rather than decorate them:
   - A truncated method table is a subset of what ran, so every count from it is
     a lower bound. `applicationsinfo`'s 6 569 is one.
   - **A truncated count is not even a stable lower bound.** Re-capturing
     `applicationsinfo` `recreate` with `--window 2` still overflowed and
     reported 6 597; with `--window 3` it overflowed and reported **3 473** —
     *fewer* than the longer window. Where the buffer fills determines what
     survives, so the number moves in both directions with the window.
   - The overflow is in the *clear-task burst*, not the window length: it
     reproduces at every window from 2 s to 5 s, and it does **not** occur in
     `applicationsinfo`'s `create` mode, whose 3 runs are clean. The confound
     figure for that app is therefore an under-estimate (§3) — the opposite
     direction from the other five.
   The other 56 batched captures all report `data-file-overflow=false` — 50 of
   them `exhaustive`, 6 `sampled@100us` — checked per file, and the check is now
   part of the coverage label rather than a note. The 4 continuous-layout
   captures report **no flag at all**, which is why they are labelled
   `unverifiable` and not `exhaustive`.
9. **A capture is only as good as its record.** `parse.py` will not label a
   trace `exhaustive` on its own: the batched layout does not record the sampling
   interval, so `mode` comes from the sidecar and a missing sidecar yields
   `undetermined (lower bound)`. A trace with no capture record is treated as
   **untrusted** and excluded, not assumed good.

## 7. The parsing artefacts, and what they did to the numbers

Two independent defects, both now closed and both tested.

**Scanning past `*end`.** The binary data section is not text, but it decodes
lossily into tab- and newline-separated runs. Scanning from `*methods` to EOF
instead of stopping at `*end`, on the six `recreate.run1` traces:

| trace | method table rows | rows if scanned to EOF | factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tk.al54.dev.badpixels` | 3 871 | 6 125 | 1.6× |
| `de.cweiske.headphoneindicator` | 4 442 | 9 655 | 2.2× |
| `org.billthefarmer.shorty` | 6 260 | 15 875 | 2.5× |
| `com.jeffliu.balancetheball` | 4 617 | 24 364 | 5.3× |
| `eu.quelltext.gita` | 4 649 | 61 025 | 13.1× |
| `com.oF2pks.applicationsinfo` | 6 545 | 85 575 | 13.1× |

Between **1.6× and 13.1×** more apparent rows than the trace actually contains.
The factor tracks the size of the binary data section, not the method table, so
it is unpredictable from the file size alone. `parse.py` stops at `*end`.

**The header read as method records.** A parser that flattens the whole file on
tabs and newlines and reads five tokens at a time consumes the header. Reproduced
on a real capture:

```python
>>> chunks = [tuple(toks[i:i+5]) for i in range(0, len(toks)-4, 5)]
>>> chunks[0]
('*version', '3', 'data-file-overflow=false', 'clock=dual', 'elapsed-time-usec=6289722')
```

The class field takes the value `3`; shift the alignment by one and it takes a
bare number out of `elapsed-time-usec=6289722`. That is the reported
`628`-as-a-class-name defect, and it is an artefact of reading the *header*, so
it lands in **every** trace and therefore in the cross-app intersection, which is
where it does the most damage.

`parse.py` defends twice over: the batched parser reads only between `*methods`
and `*end`, so the header is unreachable in principle; and every row's four
fields are validated independently, with a class name required to be a dotted
Java identifier and a signature required to be a real JVM descriptor — `628`
fails both. Each rejection has its own reason label, and the row accounting is
closed: `table_rows == distinct + duplicates + rejected`.

**Both fixes are load-bearing in the other direction too.** Validation that is
too strict *understates* coverage, which is worse for this study because it hides
methods a shim would need. Enforcing the grammar against real traces is what
surfaced three forms of *real framework method* that a first cut of the grammar
rejected. Reproduced on `eu.quelltext.gita.recreate.run1.trace` (4 649 table
rows) by re-applying the too-strict rules:

| too-strict rule | rows it rejects | what they really are |
|---|---|---|
| signature matched only the *first character* of a type | **968** | return and parameter types that are arrays of objects — `()[Ljava/lang/Object;`, `()[I`, `(I)[Landroid/content/pm/ApplicationInfo;` — and every method returning an object at all |
| method name required to start with a Java identifier | **108** | d8 nestmate accessors `-$$Nest$fgetthreadLocalHashCode` (leading `-`), desugared lambdas `lambda$onFrameDraw$0$android-view-ViewRootImpl$1` (internal `-`), and `<init>`/`<clinit>` |
| **total** | **1 076 of 4 649** | of which **1 046 are methods the finished grammar accepts** |

A nested bracket in a character class, `[L[^();]`, is not "an `L` followed by a
negated class" — it is *one* class containing `L^();`. Every method returning an
object was being dropped, which is most of the framework. On all 64 usable
traces the parser now rejects **nothing**, and `table_rows == distinct +
duplicates + rejected` holds for every one.

## 8. Failed captures

`com.jeffliu.balancetheball` previously produced a 0-byte trace that would have
read as a run with no framework calls. `capture.sh` now classifies every run from
the bytes it received, and `measure.mjs` excludes anything not `ok`:

| mode | ok | failed | failure reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `recreate` | 24 | 0 | |
| `create` | 15 | 2 | `failed-precondition-not-destroyed` (BACK consumed) |
| `teardown` | 15 | 2 | `failed-precondition-not-destroyed` (BACK consumed) |
| `idle` | 6 | 0 | |
| `startprof` | 4 | 8 | `failed-zero-bytes` |
| **total** | **64** | **12** | |

`parse.py` raises on a 0-byte file rather than returning an empty method set, and
`jaccard` returns `null` — not 1.0 — for two empty sets, so a failed capture
cannot score as perfect similarity. `measure.mjs` exits 1 when any capture
failed, so a script cannot mistake a clean-looking report for a clean run.

**Truncation is a third category, and it is not a failure.** A trace that ART
itself overflowed parses fine, is `outcome: ok`, and is a *subset* of what ran.
Counting it as a pass would be exactly the error this study is about, so every
run carries its own coverage label in `REPORT.md` and in the `--json` output.
Over the 64 usable runs:

| runs | per-run coverage label |
|---|---|
| **50** | `exhaustive` |
| **6** | `sampled@100us (lower bound)` — one `recreate-s100` run per app |
| **4** | `TRUNCATED (data-file-overflow) — lower bound` — `applicationsinfo` `recreate` runs 1–3, `teardown` run 2 |
| **4** | `overflow NOT verifiable (continuous layout) — unverifiable` — both `startprof` runs of `gita` and of `applicationsinfo` |

The last two buckets are the ones that were invisible until the harness was made
to look: **8 of 64 usable captures cannot support an `exhaustive` claim**, and
4 of those cannot even be checked.

## 9. Reproducibility

**Analysis is deterministic.** `measure.mjs` over an unchanged `trace/runs`
produces byte-identical markdown and JSON, verified:

```

json identical:     YES
```

**Capture is structurally reproducible, and the device is close to
reproducible.** The harness was re-run end to end on 3 of the 6 apps, same
commands, into a separate directory:

Identical file inventory (18 files, 3 apps × 3 runs × {trace, record}), identical
outcomes (`ok`/`exhaustive` on all 9), identical coverage labels. The traces are

confined to `trace/`.

## 10. How to re-run

```sh
# 0. the device (root, so the traced process can write /data/local/tmp)
$ANDROID_HOME/emulator/emulator -avd sub -no-window -no-audio -no-boot-anim \
    -gpu swiftshader_indirect -no-snapshot -port 5554
adb -s emulator-5554 root

# 1. capture. modes: recreate | create | teardown | idle | startprof
trace/capture.sh --apk <apk> --package <pkg> --runs 3 --mode recreate
trace/capture.sh --apk <apk> --package <pkg> --runs 3 --mode recreate --sampling 100
trace/capture.sh --apk <apk> --package <pkg> --runs 3 --mode startprof
trace/capture.sh --list-devices

# 2. report
node trace/measure.mjs --runs trace/runs --out trace/REPORT.md
node trace/measure.mjs --runs trace/runs --json          # machine-readable
node trace/measure.mjs --capture <pkg>=<apk>,<pkg>=<apk> --n 3 --mode recreate

# 3. a single trace, and its provenance
python3 trace/parse.py trace/runs/<pkg>.recreate.run1.trace --all

# 4. tests (no device needed)
python3 trace/tests/test_parse.py
node --test trace/tests/measure.test.mjs
```
